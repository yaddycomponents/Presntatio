A platform modernization. — In four acts.

Faster. Lighter. Built to scale. … Those were the instructions. — What follows is what happened.

Act One. The Weight of Growth. … Every success arrived carrying luggage.

Thirteen megabytes, on every visit. — Six seconds before anything appeared. — And fifteen filter systems — each of them slightly different.

Act Two. The Rebuild. … They began, as one does, by deleting things.

Two hundred twenty-nine commits. One thousand seven hundred sixty-eight files. — They deleted more than they wrote. — On purpose.

The application had been shipped as one enormous parcel. — It was opened, sorted, and repacked. — Two hundred forty-nine smaller ones.

This is where the weight had been living. … Four bundles, comfortable, undisturbed, — and downloaded by every single visitor.

Previously, the dashboard asked for everything. — Now it asks for what the dashboard needs. — A modest idea, arrived at late.

Without preload, the browser discovers its files one after another. — With it, it collects the important ones at once. — The page begins sooner.

Consider the sidebar. — The pointer merely rests on Inbox — it does not click — and the page's bundles begin arriving quietly in the background. — Assets only. — Zero requests to the server. — Nobody has decided anything yet.

One function. — Three hundred fifty-seven kilobytes. — Delivered faithfully, on every page load, — to people who never once used it.

The remedy was a single line. … Three hundred fifty-seven kilobytes became zero. — These are the most satisfying repairs, and the least visible.

Time to paint: halved. — The screen now arrives before the user has finished deciding to wait for it.

For years the build required twelve gigabytes of heap — and a ten-gigabyte swap file, — to keep it from collapsing. … Rolldown bundles in Rust — off the Node heap entirely. — So the swap file was deleted. The heap fell to four. — The scaffolding came down, — because the building no longer falls down.

Certain lessons were written down, — so that they would not have to be learned a second time.

The styles had been living inside the JavaScript, — recomputed in the browser on every render. … They were moved into ordinary files. — They are now computed once — by the build.

Cascade layers decide who wins. — Ours are ordered; the last one always does. … Then version six arrived with unlayered styles — outside every layer, and therefore above them all. — This was handled.

There had been fifteen filter systems. — One fix required fifteen fixes. … They became a single configuration-driven module — roughly sixty-five percent of the filter layer, simply gone.

One filter now. — The same pills, the same saved views, — across fifteen pages that had never once agreed.

It can be operated entirely by keyboard. … The people who work here all day — tend to notice this first.

Architecture that compounds. — Each thing built on it is cheaper than the thing before it.

Formerly, every tile fetched at once, whether or not you looked at it. … Now a tile fetches when it enters view. — Not before.

The list renders only what is on screen. — A thousand rows, or ten. — The browser cannot tell the difference.

Your place is kept in the address bar. — The filters, the page, the customer — all of it. … You may now send a colleague a link — and be entirely confident about what they will see.

React eighteen. Vite eight. React Query. — The foundation was replaced while the building remained open.

Four leaks were found in the startup path. — Objects created on every render, and never once closed. — They are closed now.

Act Three. The Payoff. … What all of it actually bought.

The dashboard loads in seven hundred fifty-five milliseconds. — Two and a half megabytes. — Fourteen startup calls, where there had been forty-three.

Every line moves in the same direction. … This is rarer than it sounds, — and was not guaranteed.

Two thirds less code was written. — Compression did the remaining third. … We cite the bytes that crossed the network — those are the ones the user waits for.

The build once took one minute and forty-four seconds. … It now takes fifteen. — Ninety seconds returned, on every push, — to every engineer, forever.

Some honesty, briefly. — Different deployments. Different datasets. Cold loads. — Read the direction — and not the decimals.

One filter system. — Not fifteen. … If you remember a single line, we would prefer it were that one.

It loads two and a half times faster. — The feeds no longer freeze. — Customers did not need to be told any of this.

One toolkit. Less code to own. — The team now spends its time on things that did not exist last year.

Act Four. The Agents. … A fast build introduced a new kind of mistake.

The component library now ships its own expertise. — Alfred keeps the house. He does not write code. — He determines who should. … A quick question, you look up yourself. — A real job is sent away — and reports back.

Neo reads every pull request before anyone else does. … On this one, it found a command-injection hole — in the workflow written to harden the pipeline. … One dollar and seventy-six cents. — Seven and a half minutes. — No human had opened the file.

The platform, re-engineered. … Faster, lighter, and considerably harder to break. — Thank you for your attention.
